What is Working Capital Finance?

Working capital finance refers to short-term funding solutions designed to help businesses manage their day-to-day operational expenses and maintain liquidity. This type of financing is essential for covering costs such as payroll, inventory purchases, rent, and utilities, especially during periods of fluctuating cash flow.

Common forms of working capital finance include:

  • Lines of Credit: Flexible borrowing options that allow businesses to draw funds as needed up to a predetermined limit.
  • Short-term Loans: Lump-sum loans with fixed repayment terms, typically ranging from a few months to a year.
  • Invoice Financing: Borrowing against outstanding invoices to improve cash flow while waiting for customer payments.
  • Trade Credit: Arrangements with suppliers to delay payment for goods or services, effectively providing short-term financing.

Working capital finance helps businesses maintain smooth operations, seize growth opportunities, and navigate seasonal fluctuations or unexpected expenses. It is a critical component of financial management for companies of all sizes and industries.

What is Corporate Finance?

Corporate finance focuses on strategic financial planning to help businesses raise capital, optimize debt, and improve long-term financial performance. It goes beyond simple borrowing and looks at the overall financial health of the company.

Corporate finance typically covers:

  • Business expansion funding
  • Project and CapEx financing
  • Debt restructuring and refinancing
  • Mergers & acquisitions advisory
  • Capital structure optimization

Corporate finance helps businesses grow sustainably while maintaining financial discipline and risk control.

How is corporate finance different from business loans?

Unlike standard business loans, corporate finance:

  • Focuses on structuring rather than just borrowing
  • Considers cash flow, leverage, and capital efficiency
  • Aligns funding with long-term business strategy

It is especially useful for growing companies and complex financial requirements.

What is Bridge Funding?

Bridge funding is a short-term financing solution used to bridge the gap between an immediate funding requirement and an expected future inflow, such as long-term loans, asset sales, or project revenues.

Bridge funding is commonly used for:

  • Project delays or funding gaps
  • Acquisition or takeover situations
  • Pending loan disbursements
  • Time-sensitive business opportunities

Due to its short tenure and urgency, bridge funding is typically costlier than traditional loans and requires a clear exit strategy.

Is bridge funding risky?

Bridge funding involves higher risk because:

  • It has a short repayment window
  • It depends on a defined exit plan
  • Interest rates are generally higher

However, when structured correctly, it can be a powerful tool for managing temporary liquidity needs.

What are Retail Loans?

Retail loans are financial products offered to individuals to meet personal or business-related needs. These loans are structured based on income, credit profile, and repayment capacity.

Common types of retail loans include:

  • Home loans
  • Personal loans
  • Loan Against Property (LAP)
  • Small business loans

Retail loans help individuals achieve financial goals while spreading repayment over manageable EMIs.

How does credit score impact retail loans?

A credit score reflects an individual’s creditworthiness and plays a crucial role in:

  • Loan approval chances
  • Interest rates offered
  • Loan amount eligibility

A higher credit score generally results in better loan terms, while moderate scores may still qualify under secured or alternate structures.

What is FLDG (First Loss Default Guarantee)?

FLDG is a risk-sharing arrangement where one party agrees to absorb initial losses in a loan portfolio, thereby reducing credit risk for lenders.

FLDG structures are widely used by:

  • NBFCs
  • Fintech lenders
  • Digital lending platforms

They enhance confidence, improve capital efficiency, and support faster credit expansion.

Does FLDG eliminate credit risk?

No. FLDG:

  • Covers only initial losses up to an agreed limit
  • Does not eliminate overall portfolio risk
  • Must be supported by strong underwriting and monitoring

Proper structuring and compliance are critical for effective FLDG implementation.

Each industry requires a customized working capital structure based on its operating cycle.

What are secured and unsecured retail loans?

Retail loans can be classified based on collateral requirements.

  • Secured Loans: Require collateral such as property or assets (e.g., home loans, LAP).
  • Unsecured Loans: Do not require collateral and are approved based on income and credit profile (e.g., personal loans).

Secured loans usually offer lower interest rates due to reduced lender risk.

Can self-employed individuals get retail loans?

Yes, self-employed professionals and business owners are eligible for retail loans.

Lenders typically evaluate:

  • Income stability
  • Bank statement consistency
  • Business vintage
  • Credit history

Proper financial documentation improves approval chances.

What is the role of FLDG in lending partnerships?

FLDG plays a key role in risk-sharing partnerships between lenders and originators.

  • Accelerate credit deployment

How is the FLDG percentage decided?

The FLDG coverage percentage is determined based on:

It helps:

  • Portfolio risk profile
  • Credit underwriting standards
  • Regulatory considerations

It is structured carefully to balance risk-sharing without encouraging excessive risk-taking.
